Coffee Cafe FAQ

What is the difference between espresso and brewed coffee?
Espresso is a small, concentrated coffee made by forcing pressurized water through finely ground coffee. Brewed coffee, such as drip or pour-over, uses more water and usually has a larger serving size. Espresso often tastes stronger, but a full mug of brewed coffee can contain more total caffeine because the serving is larger.

What is a latte?
A latte is espresso with steamed milk and a small layer of foam. It is usually mild, creamy, and easy to customize. Common additions include vanilla, caramel, cocoa, cinnamon, or alternative milks such as oat, soy, or almond.

What is a cappuccino?
A cappuccino is espresso with steamed milk and a thicker layer of foam than a latte. It usually tastes more coffee-forward because it has less milk in the cup. Traditional cappuccinos are smaller than many modern cafe versions.

What is an americano?
An americano is espresso diluted with hot water. It keeps some espresso character while becoming closer in volume and strength to brewed coffee. It is a useful choice for someone who wants a simple black coffee when a cafe does not serve drip coffee.

What do milk alternatives change?
Oat milk often creates a creamy texture and works well in lattes. Soy milk has more protein and can foam well, but flavor varies by brand. Almond milk tastes nutty and lighter, and it may separate if overheated. Barista-style versions are designed to steam and foam more reliably.

How should I ask for less sweetness?
Ask for fewer pumps of syrup or request sauce on the side when available. Many cafe drinks can be ordered half-sweet, unsweetened, or with a smaller amount of flavored syrup.

What should I order if I want less caffeine?
Choose decaf espresso, half-caf espresso, a smaller serving, or milk-forward drinks. Herbal tea and some steamers contain no coffee caffeine.
