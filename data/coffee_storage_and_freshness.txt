Coffee Storage and Freshness FAQ

How long does roasted coffee stay fresh?
Whole bean coffee is usually best within a few weeks after roasting. Many bags taste balanced after a short resting period of two to seven days, then slowly lose aroma. Ground coffee stales faster because more surface area is exposed to air. For best flavor, grind beans shortly before brewing.

How should I store coffee beans?
Store coffee in an airtight container away from heat, light, moisture, and strong odors. A cool cabinet is better than a sunny counter. Coffee absorbs smells easily, so keep it away from spices, cleaning products, and open food containers.

Should coffee be stored in the refrigerator?
The refrigerator is usually not ideal. It has moisture, temperature changes, and food odors that can affect coffee. Daily movement in and out of the refrigerator can also cause condensation. Room-temperature storage in a sealed container is better for coffee used within a few weeks.

Can coffee be frozen?
Freezing can work for longer storage if the coffee is sealed well and divided into small portions. Remove only the portion you need and let it warm while still sealed to avoid condensation on the beans. Do not repeatedly thaw and refreeze the same bag.

How can I tell if coffee is stale?
Stale coffee often smells flat, papery, or dusty. Brewed stale coffee may taste dull, bitter, woody, or hollow, even if the grind and brew ratio are correct. Fresh coffee usually has a clearer aroma and more distinct sweetness, acidity, or roast flavor.

Does packaging matter?
Valve bags help freshly roasted coffee release gas without letting much oxygen in. Once opened, the bag should be rolled tightly or the beans should be moved to an airtight container. Clear jars look attractive but expose coffee to light, so they are best kept inside a cabinet.
