Coffee Brewing Basics FAQ

What grind size should I use?
Grind size controls how quickly water extracts flavor from coffee. Espresso needs a fine grind because water passes through the coffee quickly under pressure. Pour-over usually uses a medium grind so water can flow steadily through the filter. French press works best with a coarse grind because the coffee steeps in water for several minutes. If coffee tastes sour or weak, try a finer grind. If it tastes harsh or bitter, try a coarser grind.

What water temperature is best?
Most brewed coffee works well with water around 90 to 96 degrees Celsius, or 195 to 205 degrees Fahrenheit. Water that is too cool can under-extract coffee and make it taste thin or sharp. Water that is too hot can emphasize bitterness, especially with darker roasts.

What brew ratio should I start with?
A common starting ratio is 1 part coffee to 15 or 16 parts water by weight. For example, use 20 grams of coffee with 300 to 320 grams of water. A stronger cup can use a 1:14 ratio. A lighter cup can use a 1:17 ratio. Measuring by weight is more consistent than measuring by scoops.

How are common brewing methods different?
Pour-over highlights clarity and aroma because water passes through a paper filter. French press creates a heavier body because coffee oils and fine particles stay in the cup. Espresso is concentrated and intense because pressure pushes water through finely ground coffee. Cold brew steeps coffee in cool water for many hours, producing a smooth drink with low perceived acidity.

How can I improve a bad cup?
Change only one variable at a time. Adjust grind size first, then brew ratio, water temperature, or contact time. Fresh beans, clean equipment, and filtered water also make a noticeable difference.
