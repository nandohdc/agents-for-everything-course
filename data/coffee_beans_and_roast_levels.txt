Coffee Beans and Roast Levels FAQ

What are the main types of coffee beans?
Most everyday coffee is made from arabica or robusta beans. Arabica is common in specialty coffee because it often tastes smoother, sweeter, and more aromatic. Robusta usually has a stronger body, more bitterness, and more caffeine. Some blends combine arabica and robusta to balance flavor, crema, cost, and strength.

How do roast levels affect flavor?
Light roast coffee usually keeps more of the bean's original character. It can taste bright, floral, fruity, or tea-like, especially when brewed as pour-over or filter coffee. Medium roast coffee balances origin flavor with sweetness and body. It often has notes like caramel, nuts, chocolate, or toasted grain. Dark roast coffee has more roast-driven flavor, with heavier body, lower perceived acidity, and notes like cocoa, smoke, or roasted nuts.

Does darker roast mean more caffeine?
Not always. Caffeine changes only slightly during roasting. If coffee is measured by scoop volume, lighter roasts can contain a bit more caffeine because the beans are denser. If measured by weight, light and dark roasts are usually similar. Robusta beans generally contain more caffeine than arabica beans, regardless of roast level.

What does single-origin mean?
Single-origin coffee comes from one country, region, farm, or cooperative. It is useful when someone wants distinct flavor notes from a specific place. Blends combine beans from multiple sources to create a consistent flavor profile, often for espresso or milk drinks.

What is decaf coffee?
Decaf coffee has most caffeine removed before roasting. It is not completely caffeine-free, but it has far less caffeine than regular coffee. Good decaf can still show roast level, body, aroma, and sweetness when brewed carefully.
